Binaural Beats for Focus: What They Are and How to Use Them

Binaural beats are a simple audio trick: play two tones at slightly different frequencies, one in each ear, and your brain perceives a slow "beat" at the difference between them. Play 300 Hz in one ear and 310 Hz in the other and you hear a pulsing 10 Hz rhythm that does not actually exist in the sound. That perceived rhythm is a binaural beat.

How the effect works

The two tones have to be delivered separately to each ear, which means stereo headphones, not speakers. Your brainstem detects the frequency difference between the two inputs and follows it, an effect called brainwave entrainment. The beat frequency you perceive is always the difference between the two tones, so changing one frequency changes the beat.

In ProgPomo the left and right ear frequencies are both adjustable, and the beat is shown live as their difference. The built-in presets map to the standard brainwave bands.

The frequency bands

BandRangeTypically linked to
Delta1–4 HzDeep sleep
Theta4–8 HzRelaxation, light meditation
Alpha8–13 HzCalm, relaxed alertness
Beta13–30 HzActive focus and concentration
Gamma30–50 HzHigh-level processing (research interest)

For focus work, alpha and low-beta are the bands people usually reach for. Delta and theta are better suited to winding down than to getting work done.

What the research actually says

Be honest: the evidence is mixed. Some small studies report improvements in attention and mood with binaural beats, while others find no measurable effect over a control tone. There is no large, repeated body of evidence proving they make you smarter or more focused. The mechanism (entrainment) is real; whether it reliably changes your state in a useful way varies from person to person.

That does not make them useless. A quiet, steady sound can act as a focus anchor even when the entrainment does nothing, much the way some people work well with white noise or lo-fi. The honest framing: treat binaural beats as an experiment. Run a session with them, run one without, and keep whichever works for you.

How to use them in ProgPomo

  • Headphones required. The effect needs one tone per ear, so use stereo headphones, not speakers.
  • Pick a band for the task. Alpha for calm, sustained work; low-beta if you want more alertness.
  • Keep the volume low. It should sit under your attention, not over it.
  • Start small. A session or two a day is plenty to judge whether they help you.
  • Auto-start with focus sessions. ProgPomo can start the beats automatically when a focus session begins.

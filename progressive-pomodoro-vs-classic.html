Pomodoro vs Progressive Pomodoro: Which Focus Timer Should You Use?

Both methods use a timer to structure work and rest. The difference is what happens to the session length. Classic Pomodoro keeps it fixed. Progressive Pomodoro lets it grow. Here is how they compare, and how to pick the one that fits you.

The classic Pomodoro method

The classic Pomodoro method, created by Francesco Cirillo in the late 1980s, is simple: 25 minutes of focused work, 5 minutes of rest, repeated in blocks of four, followed by a longer break. The fixed rhythm is its strength. It is predictable, easy to follow, and works well for people with stable focus.

Which one should you use?

Use classic Pomodoro if you already focus well in fixed blocks and value a predictable rhythm. It needs no ratings and no decisions, just start the timer.

Use the progressive version if your focus varies from day to day, if 25 minutes feels too long or too short, or if you want to deliberately build longer deep-work sessions over time. The rating system turns each session into a small experiment about how much you can handle.

There is no wrong choice. Many people start with classic Pomodoro and switch to progressive when they want to push past the 25-minute ceiling. The 2-minute warm-up makes the switch easy: the first day costs almost nothing.
